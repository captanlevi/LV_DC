# yt-H (Q am. 15 (6)): proposed table, cap, initial-state rule

peter, 2026-10-19. Design inputs only: the stationary distributions and a walk simulation. No session outcome was
seen. The numbers are in `yt_h_2026-10-19.json` (same directory). It is not in code. levi records it in the prereg,
then it goes into `utils/shaping.YOUTUBE_MATRICES` with a FROZEN_COLLECTOR row before its first session.

**Band.** yt Q_1080p's band is {9, 10}. Rung 3040 kbit/s (vp9 + audio, declared average) × 1.4 = 4256. State
midpoints: 9 = 5000, 10 = 7500, 8 = 3250.

- **Cap:** 2, as `current` / TW-M. Low states are transient here, with stationary mass in 1–3 of about 0.03.
- **Initial state:** stationary. A Q_1080p target starts in the band, drawn from the band-restricted stationary
  distribution (am. 15 (5)).

**Design.**
- Upward drift through 1–8. Long dwell in 9–10, so a band run lasts long enough for Auto to ramp to 1080p and hold.
  The mean band run is about 5 episodes, against about 2.8 under `current`.
- Two deep drops: 9 → 4 and 10 → 3, each 0.05. They put high → low switches and some stall exposure into the
  matrix's data. Otherwise every move is at most two states down.

**Properties** (JSON `matrices`):
- Stationary mass in {9, 10}: about 0.52. The am. 15 (6) floor is 0.25, and the draw threshold is 2/21. For
  comparison: `current` about 0.16, A about 0.08 (below the draw threshold), B about 0.02.
- The simulation counts the walk only. The player's ramp inside a band run is not modelled, so in-band episodes are an
  upper bound on Q_1080p minutes.

   0.40. I proposed the stronger one because yt_47's yield, not the mass, was the gap.
2. Should the deep drops stay? They help the down-switch and stall readings, but cost a little band time.
